# ModeForge Image Analyzer Notes

**Status:** S18a-S18f + S22, browser-local single-image beam-profile analyzer (v2.1 release). Gate self-calibration v5.1 plus the S20 hardening pass (gate-spec section 13) and the S22 residual-diagnostics stage; S18-R2 released-number honesty instruments.
**Datum:** 2026-08-25.

## Scope

The image analyzer is a browser-local, single-image beam-profile tool: TIFF/PNG import, raw-image diagnostics, background correction, region-of-interest (ROI) selection, second-moment analysis, cut profiles, Gaussian / elliptical-Gaussian / Super-Gaussian fits, release-gated width reporting, and JSON/CSV/PNG export. It lives in its own computation domain, `packages/image`, independent of the beamline/field/optimizer packages (it borrows only warning/validation/units vocabulary from `packages/core`). The caustic-fit tab (multi-plane M^2 fitting) is untouched by any of this - the analyzer measures one image, not a beam propagation series.

Everything below is the documented ModeForge analyzer method: an independently defined, fully public evaluation path. Every threshold, formula and gate is either a closed-form result or a number measured against synthetic ground truth in this repository, reported honestly as such, including where that method reaches its limits.

## Import and Decode Limits

Supported input: TIFF (8/16/32-bit unsigned integer and float32, uncompressed strips, multi-page with page selection, embedded metadata sanitized of control characters and capped at 4096 characters for both display and export) and PNG (grayscale 8/16-bit exact, RGB(A) 8-bit with channel selection and a false-color warning). Unsupported subsets - TIFF compression, tiling, planar storage, palette data, inverted polarity (PhotometricInterpretation "WhiteIsZero"), and any Orientation tag other than the identity (value 1); PNG Adam7 interlacing, palette, 16-bit RGB, tRNS transparency - are blocked with a precise message instead of being silently misdecoded (the same "honest blocking" policy the ZMX importer uses for materials it cannot resolve). The two TIFF metadata blocks exist because both fields would silently change the measurement itself: an uninverted WhiteIsZero frame analyzes the photographic negative, and an unapplied rotation swaps which physical pixel pitch belongs to which axis.

Decoded images are capped at 4096 x 4096 pixels; larger inputs are blocked before an allocation is attempted. Upload size is capped at 128 MB.

## Background Correction

`packages/image/src/background.ts` supports five methods: no correction, a manual constant offset, a dark-frame image (same dimensions and dtype) subtracted pixel by pixel, the median of one or more background rectangles subtracted everywhere, and a robust tilted plane fitted to one or more background rectangles and subtracted everywhere.

Corrected pixel values are never clipped at zero; the fraction of negative values after correction is reported and feeds the negative-power honesty warning below.

### The noise scale sigma_B

Every downstream consumer - the residual gate, the FWHM signal guard, the multi-peak gate, peak-to-background-noise, the gate self-calibration Monte Carlo - reads one noise scale, `sigma_B_hat`, instead of several separate roles used in earlier drafts. It is estimated on the background-CORRECTED field: an early build measured it on the raw image, which desynchronized it by orders of magnitude from every corrected-domain consumer on a sloped background, and was fixed before release.

Reference pixels for the estimate are the user's background rectangles when any were supplied (overlapping rectangles pixel-deduplicated), otherwise the documented ROI rim frame: the four border strips of the confirmed ROI, each strip's thickness 5 percent of the shorter ROI side, at least 1 pixel.

The scale is a robust cascade with a dtype-aware floor, never a plain standard-deviation fallback:

```text
robust = 1.4826 * MAD(reference pixels)
if robust == 0: robust = IQR(reference pixels) / 1.349
floor  = 0.5 counts                                   (integer dtypes)
       = 1e-12 * (P90 - P10 of the reference samples)  (float32, nearest-rank quantiles)
sigma_B_hat = max(robust, floor)
```

The reported `scaleSource` says which branch produced the value: `mad`, `iqr`, `floor`, or `zero`. `sigma_B_hat = 0` is a legitimate result for a perfectly constant reference; every quantity derived from it is then reported as 0 or null, never as Infinity.

The robust tilted plane is fitted only on background pixels (rectangles or the rim frame), with a rank/condition guard and a leverage guard: the maximum OLS leverage of any single sample is capped at 0.9, so one pixel can never alone fix the slope. The IRLS Huber delta is `1.345 * max(1.4826 * MAD(residuals), anchorFloor)`, with `anchorFloor` = 0.5 counts (integer dtypes) or, for float32, `1e-6 * (IQR + |median|)` of the samples with a smallest-nonzero-magnitude fallback - large enough that pure rounding residuals of an already-exact fit never collapse the weights, small enough that a genuine gradient step still gets down-weighted (an earlier decile-span anchor imported the scale of bright contamination and was replaced; at extreme float levels around 1e8 counts the anchor is a few float32 ulps, so a ~50-count defect there is treated as an inlier - a documented edge, not a target). The fit is capped at 50 IRLS iterations. That cap IS reachable - a bimodal reference (about half the rim pixels at a second level) ends at 50 iterations with `converged: false` - and the flag is exported; measured over 120 such scenes the worst released width error it caused was +0.14 percent, because the stage-B fit re-estimates its own background, so the consequence is a wrong exported plane and an inflated `sigma_B_hat`, not a wrong released width. A dedicated warning for the unconverged plane is on the named backlog.

Two statistical properties of the plane's noise estimate are corrected for explicitly. First, the scale is measured on the fit's own residuals, which consumes effective degrees of freedom; the exported `sigma_B_hat` therefore carries an empirically calibrated correction `c(n) = n / (n - 2.4)` (the effective df decomposes into the three plane parameters' linearized 1.5, the finite-sample bias of the MAD itself at about 0.85, and a small Huber inlier-concentration remainder; measured over single-rect, corner-cluster and edge-ring reference layouts, which agree within sampling error, so there is no layout term). Second, the correction applies exactly when the sigma reference is the same PIXEL SET the plane was fitted on - compared as a resolved pixel union, so reordering, duplicating or re-tiling the same rectangles cannot silently drop it, while a strict subset or superset (which carried the fit only in part) deliberately stays uncorrected.

Reference rectangles below 9 finite samples do not apply their method at all: the analysis degrades to no correction, the scale provenance says so (`floor` or the honest `zero`), and `IMAGE_NOISE_SCALE_SUSPECT` fires - a 1x1 "reference" on a hot pixel can therefore no longer subtract its defect from the whole image. For rectangle-median references, a linear trend across the per-rectangle medians that exceeds what the pooled in-rectangle scatter allows raises `IMAGE_BACKGROUND_GRADIENT_IN_REFERENCE` (calibrated to 0 false fires in 20 000 null realizations per control geometry): the honest signal that a flat-offset method was chosen for a sloped background.

## Region of Interest

Three ways to set the analysis domain: the full frame, a manually typed rectangle, or a suggested ROI computed from the corrected image - every pixel above `k * sigma_B_hat` (k = 4 default) is masked, the largest 4-connected component wins, and its bounding box is padded and clamped to the image. A mask covering more than 25 percent of the frame is flagged noise-suspect only when the sigma behind the threshold is not an actual measurement (`scaleSource` is `floor` or `zero`); an HDR beam that legitimately fills a third of the frame under a measured sigma is not flagged. The suggestion is only ever a proposal - nothing downstream trusts an unconfirmed ROI.

The padding is derived from the mask, per axis, and is reported as `paddingXPx` and `paddingYPx` - those are the authoritative padding numbers. `paddingPx` is the BASE border (8 px by default): it is the floor of the derived padding, and it is the exact padding whenever a caller passes `paddingPx` explicitly. A fixed border was a constant while the aperture the clipping gate checks grows with the beam, which made the suggestion a dead end - applying it turned a releasing frame into `aperture_clipped`, and the suggestion computed inside that ROI reproduced itself.

The derivation inverts the mask edge. A Gaussian crosses the threshold at `r = sigma * sqrt(2 * ln(peak / threshold))`, so the per-axis half extent of the mask bounding box reads back a beam size, `sigmaEst_a = halfExtent_a / sqrt(2 * ln(peak_robust / threshold))`, reported as `sigmaEstXPx` and `sigmaEstYPx`. The padding is then the largest of three terms: the base border, `ceil(1.25 * 6 * sigmaEst_a - halfExtent_a)` (aiming the suggested half side at 1.25 times the 6-sigma check ellipse), and `ceil(2 * halfExtent_a)` (a floor on the mask extent itself, for profiles whose edge is steeper than a Gaussian's and whose true size the inversion therefore under-reads). `peak_robust` is the largest 3x3 median inside the component, not the raw maximum: a hot pixel welded to the beam would otherwise raise the ratio, shrink `sigmaEst` and shrink the padding. Three guards fall back to the base border instead of inverting: a zero threshold, a peak-to-threshold ratio at or below `sqrt(e)`, and a degenerate estimate. `sigmaEstXPx` / `sigmaEstYPx` are `null` in exactly those cases and whenever `paddingPx` was passed explicitly.

One case earlier than any of those: `k` and `sigma_B_hat` are each finite, but their product can round to `Infinity` (a sigma near `Number.MAX_VALUE` is enough). There is then no suggestion at all - `null`, not a fallback rectangle - and that is arithmetic rather than a special case: a product above `Number.MAX_VALUE` is above every representable pixel value too, so the mask is genuinely empty. It follows that a non-finite threshold never reaches the padding guards, which is why they only test for a positive one.

`clampedToImage` says the padded rectangle did not fit the frame and was cut back. Since the padding now asks for a definite aperture rather than a fixed border, this reads as "the frame could not give the suggestion the aperture it asked for" - which is also the situation in which applying the suggestion may still be suppressed as `aperture_clipped`, honestly, because the beam sits too close to the image edge for any rectangle inside this frame to help.

Whichever ROI is confirmed for the run - full frame, rectangle, or an accepted suggestion - becomes the ROI the release gates and both moment stages read.

### ROI stability sweep (a stage-A indicator)

The background is fitted once, on the primary configuration; the sweep then varies only the analysis geometry over that statically corrected image, never re-fitting the background per variant. Variants: size factors 0.8 / 0.9 / 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.2 and center shifts of +-5 percent (physical when calibration is set); a variant that would leave the image is clamped, shrunk, and flagged. On a full-frame base ROI only shrink variants exist and the sweep is labeled size-only; "undeterminable" is then the normal, expected outcome (a full frame has nowhere to grow), not an error. Sensitivity is computed only over valid variants; fewer than three valid variants also reports undeterminable. This sweep is a stage-A indicator only; the stability of the stage-B release is tracked separately, through the alpha-consistency delta described below.

## Three-Tier Width Reporting

The analyzer never reports one number under the name "the beam width." It reports three, on strictly separated footing, and never substitutes one for another:

1. **Stage A - ROI moments (diagnostic)**: signed second moments over the confirmed ROI, computed the classical way. Labeled a diagnostic because it is one: measured on a typical-SNR full frame it is invalid roughly 78 percent of the time, and the runs that survive are biased median +121 to +221 percent high (background and every off-beam pixel inside the ROI carries weight in a plain moment sum). It still drives the ROI stability sweep above.
2. **Stage B - aperture moments, the released headline value**: second moments computed only inside an ellipse around a 2D-Gaussian fit centre. This is the UI's "D4sigma (ellipse)" / "D4sigma (Ellipse)" value, and the only one that is release-gated. It is null - never silently backfilled - unless every gate in the next section passes.
3. **Stage C - fit width, `4 * sigma_fit`**: the model-bound width of the 2D-Gaussian fit itself. For genuinely Gaussian-core beams this is the most precise of the three - its repeatability (standard deviation across repeated measurements) runs 3-4x tighter than stage B's - but it is a MODEL number, not a moment measurement, and is labeled "fit width 4sigma" rather than folded into the headline value.

**The hard non-substitution rule:** when stage B is suppressed, the analyzer reports the suppression reason in place of a number. It never falls back to stage A or stage C to fill the headline slot, in the UI, the JSON export, or the CSV export - the field name itself carries the tier semantics, so a diagnostic value can never travel under a released label. A number the analyzer is not confident in must never look like one it is.

## The Aperture-Moments Method (Stage B)

The aperture's semi-axes are `alpha * sigmaMajorFit` and `alpha * sigmaMinorFit` (alpha = 4 by default) around the centre of a 2D-Gaussian fit. The aperture is always built from the plain 2D-Gaussian fit, even when a Super-Gaussian fit is also requested and shown: an oversized aperture on a good beam costs almost nothing, an undersized one costs a great deal, and only the Gaussian fit gives a stable, well-understood sigma to scale from. The moment reference point is the centroid measured INSIDE the aperture, a second small refinement pass, not the fit centre itself.

For a pure Gaussian at alpha = 4, a closed-form result applies: the aperture captures a second-moment truncation factor of 0.9986568 of the infinite-domain moment (pinned by the moments oracle). Taken alone, a finite aperture can only exclude moment weight relative to the true infinite-domain integral, which would read low. Measured end-to-end against synthetic ground truth, the realized median bias for well-resolved beams is -0.14 to -0.36 percent and ROI-independent - the aperture reconstructs D4sigma correctly under full-frame and low-SNR conditions where stage A demonstrably fails. A later cross-verification pass reproduced this directly on a 200x160 synthetic frame (sigma 11/6 px, theta 0.7 rad): released D4sigma -0.317 / -0.166 percent, and -0.234 / -0.249 percent once mapped to physical units with the truncation factor folded in - inside the documented band.

That truncation bias is not the whole picture, and it does not make the released width a one-sided quantity. A second, independent effect runs the other way at narrow widths: under pixel-area integration, a released axis whose sigma sits below about 3 px reads systematically HIGH instead of low (measured +1.0 percent at sigma 2 px, +4 percent at sigma 1 px - a quantization effect of integrating a continuous Gaussian over discrete pixel areas, distinct from the aperture-truncation bias above and not corrected for). Below that 3 px threshold the analyzer raises an info-level `IMAGE_WIDTH_RESOLUTION_LIMIT` warning naming the affected axis and its approximate bias. So the honest statement is narrower than "reads low by construction": a well-resolved D4sigma reads a fraction of a percent low, but a narrow, only just-resolved one can read high instead - the net direction is not fixed by aperture truncation alone.

## Release Gates (Stage B)

Stage B moments are null unless every gate below passes, in this precedence - the FIRST failing gate is the one reported as `suppressionReason`, even when a later gate would also fail:

1. **Fit convergence** (`fit_not_converged`): the 2D-Gaussian fit must report status `converged`.
2. **Positive amplitude** (`nonpositive_amplitude`): the fitted amplitude must be greater than 0.
3. **Noise-relative residual** (`residual_high`): the full-resolution model RMS over the ROI must not exceed `max(2 * sigma_B_hat, 0.005 * peakCorrected)`. An earlier, peak-relative 5 percent threshold was itself a noise detector at SNR 20 - a pure-noise beam sits near peak/20, which the old rule would have flagged as a bad fit.
4. **Aperture clipping** (`aperture_clipped`): a LARGER check ellipse (alpha = 6 semi-axes, same centre and orientation) must lie fully inside the CONFIRMED ROI, not merely the image. If the check ellipse leaves the ROI, both alpha passes clip at the same boundary, so their ratio stays deceptively stable while both are equally wrong - the release is suppressed rather than trusting a ratio between two truncated apertures.
5. **Aperture coverage** (`coverage_insufficient`): when non-finite pixels intersect the measurement aperture, the fitted beam model momented with and without the observed mask must not differ by more than 2.0 percent on either axis, and at least 20 percent of the aperture must carry finite data - see the coverage entry in the warnings section.
6. **Alpha consistency** (`alpha_inconsistent`): see self-calibration below.
7. **Multi-peak** (`multi_peak`): see self-calibration below.

Gates 3-7 read a background-subtracted stage-B field: the fitted background plane (constant plus any tilted slopes) is removed from the ROI before the two ellipse-moment passes and the pedestal scan run, so a residual constant or tilted background the fit already accounts for cannot itself trip the alpha gate. The residual gate is the one exception - it keeps comparing the original corrected values against the full model, background included.

### Residual pedestal indicator

Independent of the release gates, the analyzer reports a pedestal hint: the mean corrected intensity OUTSIDE the aperture ellipse but inside the ROI, relative to the corrected peak (`meanOutsideRelativeToPeak`), and separately the fitted background level itself relative to the peak (`fittedBackgroundRelativeToPeak`). Either exceeding 0.5 percent of the peak raises the hint ("a background pedestal may bias the reported widths; check the background correction"). The two figures exist for different reasons. Because the two ellipse-moment passes run on the fit-background-subtracted stage-B field, a genuinely FLAT pedestal is absorbed by the fit and removed before the moments are taken: measured, a 1 percent flat pedestal leaves the released D4sigma biased by only -0.001 percent, not the double-digit figure an earlier measurement once reported. `fittedBackgroundRelativeToPeak` is what makes that absorbed level visible again - a background correction that leans on the fit rather than a clean subtraction still raises the hint even though D4sigma itself is nearly immune to it. `meanOutsideRelativeToPeak` catches the complementary case, an outside-aperture residual the fit did NOT absorb into its constant term.

## S18 v5.1: Self-Calibrating Gates

Gates 5 and 6 were originally fixed thresholds, calibrated noise-free. At realistic noise they turned out to be detectors for noise itself rather than for genuine model mismatch: measured on a 300x240 synthetic frame (sigma 11x6 / 10x6), a PERFECT Gaussian released 15/15 seeds at SNR 100, but the fixed design suppressed 24 of 25 seeds at SNR 20 (a perfect Gaussian released only about once in 25 tries). Two measured causes: the alpha-consistency delta of pure noise reaches 2.1-4.9 percent against a fixed 3 percent ceiling calibrated noise-free, and the multi-peak candidate floor (a fixed `4 * sigma_B`) sat below the expected maximum of the roughly 72000 iid noise samples in a typical ROI, so pure noise alone produced two or more "peaks" with about 67 percent probability.

The fix, adjudicated from two independent statistical derivations and approved before landing: the analyzer computes each gate's own null distribution for THIS image, at analysis time, instead of comparing against a number calibrated once on noise-free synthetics.

### Alpha consistency: per-image, per-axis Monte Carlo null

The observed statistic is unchanged - per axis, `100 * |D4sigma(alpha=4) - D4sigma(alpha=6)| / D4sigma(alpha=6)` on the stage-B (background-subtracted) field, both passes through the production moment code. Only the CEILING changes, from a fixed 3 percent to a per-axis, per-image threshold; 3 percent remains a hard floor, so self-calibration can only ever be as strict as the noise-free design, never stricter.

The null model freezes the fitted geometry (centre, sigmas, theta, amplitude) and adds iid `N(0, sigma_B_hat)` noise, with no background/plane term (stage B already removed it), evaluated over the alpha = 6 ellipse support. Because gate 4 has already passed when this gate runs, that ellipse sits fully inside the ROI, so the Monte Carlo runs on a small LOCAL bounding box of it (an exact coordinate translation) rather than the full image.

Runtime is bounded by power-of-two decimation targeting a decimated major sigma near 10 px:

```text
b = 1                                    if sigmaMajorPx <= 10
b = 2 ^ ceil(log2(sigmaMajorPx / 10))    otherwise
while b > 1 and sigmaMinorPx / b < 1.5: b = b / 2      (minor-axis guard)
sigmaB' = sigmaB / b     (exact: the mean of b^2 iid N(0, sigma) samples is N(0, sigma / b))
```

64 realizations are drawn with a deterministic mulberry32 generator, one independent stream per realization (fixed literal seed, never image-derived, so identical `(params, sigma_B, ROI)` give bit-identical thresholds; the first 32 realizations are bit-identical regardless of the total count, a stable prefix for debugging), using a Box-Muller transform with `u1 = 1 - rand()` so `ln(0)` is never hit. Each realization runs both ellipse-moment passes (the alpha pass and the alpha = 6 check pass) and collects the per-axis delta; fewer than 32 valid realizations fails the gate closed (`inconsistent = true`). The null delta is half-normal to measured precision, so the threshold is built from the stable low-order statistic (the RMS) rather than a noisy small-sample order statistic:

```text
thresholdMajorPercent = max(3, 2.2 * rms(deltaMajor over valid realizations))
thresholdMinorPercent = max(3, 2.2 * rms(deltaMinor over valid realizations))
```

Thresholds are computed PER AXIS and never pooled: on isotropic beams the minor-axis null RMS runs up to 1.57x the major-axis one (the same order-statistics inflation of the smaller eigenvalue that the orientation-instability warning tracks), while anisotropic beams stay balanced. `sigma_B_hat <= 0` skips the Monte Carlo entirely and the gate behaves exactly as the historical fixed-threshold design (threshold exactly 3 percent).

### Multi-peak: analytic extreme-value floor

The candidate threshold - which local maxima even qualify as peak candidates, before the greedy pairwise-separation filter - tracks the expected maximum of M iid `N(0, sigma_B_hat)` samples, with M the scanned ROI pixel count:

```text
evtThreshold = sigma_B_hat * (sqrt(2 * ln(M)) + 0.5)
candidateThreshold = max(evtThreshold, 0.1 * peakCorrected)
```

The 10-percent-of-peak floor keeps the gate meaningful when `sigma_B_hat = 0` (no measured noise scale); without it, benign flank noise on a single real beam could saturate the peak count. Everything past candidate selection is unchanged: strict 8-neighbour local maxima, sorted by value descending, accepted greedily when farther than `2 * wEst` (with `wEst = 2 * sigmaMajorFit`) from every already-accepted peak.

### Determinism

Both self-calibration paths use only fixed literal seeds, never anything image-derived, and iterate in a fixed order (realizations ascending, grid row-major). Two assessments of identical `(params, sigma_B, ROI)` return strictly equal thresholds - not merely close, bit-identical - so re-analyzing the same file reproduces the same release decision and the same numbers, every time.

One honest qualifier: "bit-identical" holds per platform. Across operating systems `Math.pow` is resolved by the platform math library and can differ in the last bit, which moves the final bits of the iterative 2D fit and of the fit-derived diagnostics (measured: relative differences at the 1e-12..1e-11 level in `fits`/`metrics`, every other result section bit-identical between Windows and Linux on the same engine and inputs). All released rounded numbers are unaffected; a `pow`-free fit formulation is on the backlog.

## The Warning Layer's Honesty Role

Warnings are the analyzer's second honesty channel, alongside gate suppression: they tell the user when a released or diagnostic number should be read with caution, without ever silently adjusting the number itself. Two warnings were themselves recalibrated in v5.1, for the same reason the gates were - a fixed threshold cannot be right across the range of ROI geometries and noise levels the analyzer sees.

**IMAGE_NEGATIVE_POWER** - a correctly background-corrected scene MUST show some negative power (a zero-mean background fluctuates below zero as often as above it), so the firing threshold adapts to what this image's own geometry and noise predict:

```text
expectedRatio = (roiPixelCount * sigma_B_hat) / (sqrt(2 * pi) * totalPositiveCounts)
fires iff negativePowerRatio > max(0.02, 1.20 * expectedRatio)
```

The 1.20 margin over the derived expectation was verified to about 3 percent (measured-to-predicted ratio 0.97, checked across a range of ROI geometries). The 0.02 floor stays in force when `sigma_B_hat = 0` (a systematic pedestal with no noise scale to derive a ratio from). Honest limit, documented: under-subtraction REDUCES negative power, so this warning structurally cannot catch that direction - the pedestal hint above covers it instead.

The same `expectedRatio` also predicts a second failure mode: when it is large, the radial power sum itself is substantially noise, which inflates the encircled-power radii the radial-distribution readout reports (spurious positive noise power far from the beam pushes r50/r95/etc. past their true values). **IMAGE_RADIAL_NOISE_DOMINATED** fires when `expectedRatio` exceeds 15 percent, calibrated between a dominated case (a sigma-6 beam on a 121x121 ROI at SNR 20, measured r95 +368 percent high, MUST warn) and a healthy case (a well-contained SNR-100 beam, radii measured within about 2 percent, MUST stay silent).

**IMAGE_ROI_SENSITIVE** - watches the MAJOR axis only: the minor-axis half-spread is noise-dominated regardless of any constant (measured p95 21 percent at SNR 100, consistent with the same 1.57x minor-axis inflation the alpha-consistency threshold accounts for), with an adaptive noise floor:

```text
noiseFloorPercent = 8 * 100 / peakToBackgroundNoise      (0 when that ratio is unavailable)
fires iff majorAxisHalfSpreadPercent > max(5, noiseFloorPercent)
```

On a full-frame base ROI the original design simply never fired this warning, reasoning that a shrink-only sweep on a noise-filled full frame is ROI-dependent by construction and that `IMAGE_ROI_UNDETERMINABLE` already said so. That reasoning did not hold up: a healthy full-frame sweep yields exactly three valid shrink variants, so `IMAGE_ROI_UNDETERMINABLE` never actually covers it, and a real excess went unreported - measured half-spreads of 6.3-25.5 percent released with zero warnings, flipping to a warning the moment the ROI shrank by one pixel. A full-frame excess is now reported too, at INFO rather than WARNING severity, with a message stating the width is ROI-dependent by construction on a full-frame base ROI and that a beam-tight ROI should be confirmed; a clean full-frame scene still stays silent.

### Other warnings (brief)

`IMAGE_FLOAT_SPECIALS` (non-finite pixels present - each statistic skips them, which SHIFTS a statistic whose support becomes asymmetric; the coverage gate below is what makes that shift safe to release), `coverage_insufficient` as a fifth suppression reason with its companion notice `IMAGE_COVERAGE_LOSS` (when non-finite pixels intersect the measurement aperture, the fitted beam model is momented with and without the observed mask; the difference IS the induced width error - structured masks such as dead sensor columns that previously released up to +53.65 percent wrong now suppress, scattered random defects that measure benign still release, and a sub-threshold measurable bias is reported as INFO), `IMAGE_ALPHA_GATE_WEAK`, `IMAGE_WING_PROBE_REDUCED` and `IMAGE_TIER_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE` (three INFO-severity honesty notices on released frames: the alpha ceiling exceeded 10 percent so the consistency test had no discriminating power at this noise level; the 9- and 12-sigma wing probes did not fit inside the ROI so the wing detector ran short-range only; the cross-tier check could not run, with its typed reason - together they close the class of failures where the honesty instruments themselves went quietly out of service), `IMAGE_BACKGROUND_GRADIENT_IN_REFERENCE` (see the background section), `IMAGE_HOT_PIXELS` and `IMAGE_SATURATION` (raw-diagnostics honesty), `IMAGE_CLIPPING_SUSPECT` (a plateau of pixels tied at the frame maximum sits below the dtype's saturation limit - a sensor-clipping heuristic, deliberately disjoint from `IMAGE_SATURATION`), `IMAGE_EDGE_TOUCH` (profile reaches the image border; now carries its own extreme-value noise term, so it no longer fires on close to 100 percent of clean SNR-20 scenes the way a plain fixed fraction once did), `IMAGE_NOISE_SCALE_SUSPECT` (`sigma_B_hat` rests on a quantization floor or zero rather than a measured scale), `IMAGE_MOMENTS_UNDEFINED` (stage A invalid, with its reason), `IMAGE_APERTURE_SUPPRESSED` (stage B suppressed, with its `suppressionReason`), `IMAGE_RESIDUAL_HIGH`, `IMAGE_MULTI_PEAK`, `IMAGE_PEDESTAL_HINT`, `IMAGE_ORIENTATION_UNSTABLE` (orientation contrast q below 0.05 - a near-circular beam's major-axis angle is then unstable to rounding-level covariance change; on a CALIBRATED frame the test runs on the physical covariance and the message quotes that number, `orientationContrastQPhysical`, so a beam that is round in micrometers is flagged even where an anisotropic pixel pitch leaves it looking elongated in pixels - measured, a 12x6 px ellipse reads q = 0.602 in pixel space and q = 0.0037 physically under a 2/4 um pitch. A square pitch scales both covariance axes alike, so the physical number equals the pixel one exactly and the verdict is unchanged; without a calibration the pixel test and its wording stand as before), `IMAGE_AXIS_NOT_RESOLVED` (a released minor-axis sigma below 1 px - not resolved at all), `IMAGE_WIDTH_RESOLUTION_LIMIT` (a released axis sigma below 3 px reads systematically high under pixel-area integration instead - a softer, wider companion to AXIS_NOT_RESOLVED that can co-fire with it; see the aperture-moments section above), `IMAGE_FWHM_AMBIGUOUS` (a second lobe touches the half-max threshold), `IMAGE_FIT_NOT_CONVERGED`. All are plain, product-neutral English, and every one names the number or condition that triggered it - none of them is decorative. Three further warnings speak only about a number that WAS released, rather than about the raw file or the fit itself - `IMAGE_ABSORBED_POWER`, `IMAGE_TIER_DISAGREEMENT`, `IMAGE_WIDTH_SCATTER` - documented in their own section below.

A final adversarial review found that a RELEASED stage-B number could still be silently wrong in two ways a gate-pass/fail verdict cannot show: the alpha-consistency gate compares two apertures on the SAME image and the SAME noise realization, so it cannot see how far the released number itself would move under a different noise draw; and a faint wide wing can be absorbed whole into the fit's flat background term, invisible to a gate that only ever looks at a uniformly-subtracted field. Three instruments close that gap. None of them is a gate - they never suppress a release - and all three speak only about a number that WAS released; a suppressed frame is already covered by `IMAGE_APERTURE_SUPPRESSED`.

### Released-width noise scatter

The alpha-consistency Monte Carlo (above) already produces one alpha-pass D4sigma per null realization. Their relative sample standard deviation, mapped back to full resolution and expressed as a percent of the observed released value, is exported as `gates.alphaConsistency.d4ScatterMajorPercent` / `d4ScatterMinorPercent` - the per-image noise scatter of the RELEASED width itself, not of the gate's pass/fail delta. Measured at SNR 20: roughly -2..+3 percent seed-to-seed scatter on a well-resolved sigma 11x6 beam, widening to -8..+5 percent at sigma 6x3 and -21..+13 percent at sigma 3x1.5 - the same beam, the same analysis, different noise draws. The exported figure tracks the true error well UNDER THE ANALYZER'S NOISE MODEL, which is additive and Gaussian with one background-derived scale: measured over released scenes in that regime, `|true error| <= 2x the exported scatter` on 96 percent of them. On shot-noise-dominated frames - a bright peak whose photon noise dwarfs the background noise the scale was estimated from - the exported scatter understates the real seed-to-seed variation by up to two orders of magnitude (measured 9.6x at peak 10000 over read noise 1, and worse as read noise shrinks), because the Monte Carlo injects only the background scale everywhere. The released WIDTH itself stays accurate there (the residual gate's peak-relative arm and the fit are unaffected in the regimes measured); it is the exported UNCERTAINTY that loses its meaning. A signal-dependent noise term needs the camera gain, which a single frame does not carry - this is an open, documented limit, not a claim. `IMAGE_WIDTH_SCATTER` fires when either axis's scatter exceeds 5 percent, stating both axis figures directly; it stays silent on every well-resolved geometry in the calibration set and fires only on the marginal ones where the uncertainty is real.

### Absorbed power and cross-tier disagreement

A faint, wide wing is invisible to the alpha gate by construction: the single-Gaussian fit absorbs it whole into its constant background term, and both alpha passes then see the same uniformly-subtracted field. Measured example: a halo at 0.05 percent of the peak amplitude and 8x the core width - 3.2 percent of the total power - released with D4sigma reading 41.6 percent low and zero warnings of any kind.

The analyzer now probes this directly. `aperture.absorbedPower` sums the residual (data minus the FULL fitted model, background included) over four concentric ellipse probes at alpha 4/6/9/12 sigma, as a fraction of the fitted Gaussian's analytic power; a flat pedestal the fit absorbed cancels exactly in that sum (it is part of the model), but an absorbed WING does not, because the wing is concentrated on the beam while its absorbed compensation is spread over the whole ROI. `IMAGE_ABSORBED_POWER` fires when the largest such excess, relative to this image's own noise-derived ceiling, clears it - measured 0 false positives over 74 clean released reference scenes, and firing in 6 of 6 seeds on the camera-realistic version of the attack scene above. A companion statistic, `flatFractionOfBeamPower`, asks the same question in the wrong reference frame (a flat pedestal against the peak) and is exported for reference only: measured over the same 74 scenes it would fire on 24 percent of them, because a harmless flat offset the fit already subtracts produces exactly the same number as an absorbed wing. It never triggers anything itself.

`IMAGE_TIER_DISAGREEMENT` looks at the same failure from the other side: whenever the stage-A diagnostic moments are plausible (the same predicate the fit's moment-refined start uses - valid, centroid inside the ROI, sigma well inside the ROI's extent), a per-axis gap between the stage-A D4sigma and the released stage-B D4sigma beyond this ROI's own noise-derived ceiling is reported directly - measured 1 false positive of 74 clean released reference scenes, against 15 of 74 for a simpler single-radial-scale version that was tried and rejected because it understated the minor axis.

Documented limit, stated honestly: at SNR 20 a wing worth about 3 percent of the beam's power is only around a 1-sigma effect against this image's own noise, and can evade both instruments - firing there would mean firing on noise, not on a real defect. What remains honest about that scene is the width-scatter warning above: a wing that evades both detectors still widens the released number's own noise scatter.

Two further loss zones of these instruments are now visible instead of silent. Their sensitivity is ROI- and noise-dependent: a tight ROI removes the 9- and 12-sigma probes the wing detector is most sensitive with (on the reference wing scene the detection excess falls from 1.73 percent at a full frame to 0.07 percent at a 100-px ROI), and a large `sigma_B_hat` widens the self-calibrated alpha ceiling to where the consistency test accepts almost anything (measured 159 percent on the minor axis of a haloed scene; on legitimately marginal low-signal geometries the canonical ceilings themselves run into the several-hundred-percent range). Neither condition is suppressed - a hard ceiling cap was measured to destroy legitimate low-signal release rates without separating the pathological cases, whose ceilings sit inside the legitimate band - but both now announce themselves on released frames (`IMAGE_WING_PROBE_REDUCED`, `IMAGE_ALPHA_GATE_WEAK`), and the cross-tier check reports when it could not run at all (`IMAGE_TIER_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`) instead of silently switching itself off exactly where the fault grows. Two small register notes for completeness: the compact-ROI rim-contamination arm of `IMAGE_NOISE_SCALE_SUSPECT` triggers below 6 fitted minor sigmas, a region the clipping gate already keeps released frames out of - on released frames that arm is effectively dead and is kept for suppressed-frame diagnostics; and explicit user background rectangles bypass both rim arms by design, since the rim is then not the reference being used.

## Fit Engine (Stage C and the Stage-B Fit)

`packages/image/src/fit.ts` is a self-contained Levenberg-Marquardt implementation: analytic Jacobians, Marquardt damping, per-parameter scaling so the normal equations stay conditioned regardless of the data's physical units, a 30-iteration cap plus a wall-clock time budget, and an honestly reported status (`converged`, `max_iterations`, `time_budget_exceeded`, `singular_normal_equations`, `invalid_start`). Three models are provided: a 1D Gaussian, a rotated elliptical 2D Gaussian with a constant or affine (tilted) background, and an exact 2D Super-Gaussian (`I = B + A * exp(-2 * ((u/w1)^2 + (v/w2)^2)^n)`, exponent clamped to [0.5, 10]) that matches the circular-beam reference formula used elsewhere in ModeForge. Converting a Super-Gaussian's `w` to an equivalent `sigma` uses the Gamma function and is kept test-only, verified against an independent Lanczos implementation.

Start values are deliberately MOMENT-FREE and robust: the corrected peak position plus a half-max-area estimate for sigma and amplitude. A moment-based start was tried first and dropped - on a full-frame ROI at typical SNR it converged only a small fraction of the time, where the moment-free start converges the large majority of runs (including full-frame) in a handful of iterations. Any ROI-moment refinement of the start point is gated by plausibility (the centroid must sit inside the ROI, the estimated sigma well inside the ROI's extent) so a background-dominated scene cannot seed a runaway fit that "converges" on an absurd result outside the image.

A ROI wider or taller than 512 px is mean-pooled by the smallest power-of-two factor `b` that brings both extents to 512 px or below before the fit runs. Reported widths are corrected back to full resolution: `sigma_full = sqrt((b * sigma_dec)^2 - b^2/12)` (equivalently `w_full = sqrt(w_fit^2 - b^2/3)` in width terms), and positions map back as `x_full = b * x_dec + (b - 1) / 2`. Decimated fits carry an informational flag rather than hiding the resolution change.

## Residual Diagnostics

For each fitted model the analyzer reports the pixel residual R = I - I_fit, the difference between the corrected image and that model's value at the same pixel, evaluated at full resolution over every finite pixel of the confirmed ROI. The Gaussian residual is available whenever the residuals section itself exists at all (whenever the Gaussian fit has parameters); the Super-Gaussian residual exists only when the Super-Gaussian fit has itself reported `converged` - a fit that stopped at `max_iterations` or any other non-converged status carries no residual diagnostics of its own, the same non-substitution discipline the released width follows.

Two normalized summaries reduce the residual field to a single number each. NRMSE is RMS(R) / A_ref, and RMS_sigma is RMS(R) / sigma_B_hat. The denominator of NRMSE, A_ref, is always the GAUSSIAN fit's amplitude - even inside the Super-Gaussian's own NRMSE - so the two models' NRMSE values sit on one shared reference and stay directly comparable side by side; a per-model amplitude would make the pair harder to read together for a marginal gain in local accuracy. NRMSE is null unless A_ref is finite and strictly positive. RMS_sigma is null unless sigma_B_hat clears the same usability bar the rest of the analyzer applies to it: the estimate must be strictly positive, its `scaleSource` must not be `zero`, and no floor must have been applied to it.

Every quantity in this section - the residual statistics and histograms below, and NRMSE/RMS_sigma themselves - is a FULL-RESOLUTION, finite-ROI quantity: it walks every finite pixel of the confirmed ROI, not the decimated grid the Levenberg-Marquardt fit itself optimizes against. The fit stage separately reports its own residual RMS over that decimated fit domain, inside the fit result itself; the two numbers answer different questions on different domains and are never interchanged. Every residual figure displayed anywhere in the analyzer names which of the two domains it comes from.

### Residual Moments

Beyond the two normalized summaries, the analyzer reports a small moment set per model: the mean, the RMS, the standard deviation, the skewness and the excess kurtosis of the residual field, plus the number of finite pixels the set was built from. RMS and the standard deviation are two distinct quantities and are exported as two distinct numbers - RMS = sqrt(E[R^2]) is the root-mean-square around zero, sigma = sqrt(E[(R - mean)^2]) is the root-mean-square around the residual field's own mean. On most converged fits the two sit close together, since the mean residual is usually small, but nothing here assumes that: both are reported honestly rather than one standing in for the other. Skewness and excess kurtosis - the fourth standardized central moment minus 3, so a normal distribution reads 0 - are population estimators, computed directly from the accumulated moments with no small-sample bias correction. For numerical stability the higher central moments are accumulated about a shifted origin rather than raw about zero; this changes nothing about the reported value, only how the sums avoid cancellation. The full moment set is suppressed (null) when the ROI's finite-pixel count is zero; skewness and excess kurtosis individually fall back to null whenever sigma is zero, since both are ratios with sigma in the denominator.

### Residual Histogram

Each model's residual histogram uses 65 bins on symmetric fixed edges at +/- 8 * sigma_B_hat, with a dedicated underflow and a dedicated overflow counter for whatever residual falls outside them. The edges are fixed BEFORE the residual field is walked - sigma_B_hat is already known at that point, independent of the residuals themselves - which is what makes a genuine single accumulation pass possible; the bins actually populated are exactly the exported edge array, not a separately recomputed width. The Gaussian and Super-Gaussian histograms, when the Super-Gaussian one exists, always share the identical edge set, so the two shapes can be read directly against each other rather than against two different scales. When sigma_B_hat does not clear the usability bar above, edges cannot be derived that way; the analyzer then declares a second full-resolution pass, walking the residual field once more to find the largest |R| across both models and building symmetric edges from that observed maximum instead - a stated, deliberate second pass, not a silently different construction hidden behind the same field names.

### Reading the Residual Map

The residual map itself displays block means, not individual pixels: neighboring pixels are averaged into display blocks before being drawn, at a block size the analyzer derives from the ROI's own size. The counts, %-peak and sigma_B_hat display modes are linear rescalings of those same block values - which value gets scaled, and when, does not change the map's numbers. What DOES change with block size is how the "one sigma_B_hat" noise band on the map should be read: on a full b-by-b block of finite pixels, pure uncorrelated background noise averages down to roughly sigma_B_hat / b, not sigma_B_hat itself, and a partial block at the ROI's edge - fewer than b^2 finite pixels contributing to the average - scatters more than that, roughly as sigma_B_hat over the square root of however many pixels actually contributed. A block reading near 1 in sigma_B_hat units is therefore not automatically a flagged deviation, and a quieter-looking block is not automatically clean. The binding sigma-based read of the residual field is the full-resolution statistics and histogram above, evaluated pixel by pixel; the map is a visual overview of the same field, not the number to interpret quantitatively on its own.

### Model Comparison Without an Information Criterion

The analyzer does not compute an information criterion (such as AIC or BIC) to choose between the Gaussian and Super-Gaussian fits, and this is a deliberate omission rather than an oversight. The Super-Gaussian model nests the Gaussian exactly at exponent n = 1, so its residual sum of squares can never exceed the Gaussian's; on a pixel-sized sample count, the usual complexity penalty such a criterion subtracts is swamped by that same sample count multiplying the log of the (always favorable) residual-sum ratio, so a criterion built this way would favor the Super-Gaussian on very nearly every image regardless of whether the extra shape freedom is real, and would do no more than restate the existing relative RMS reduction in a less transparent form. Instead the honest discriminators the analyzer reports are the fitted exponent n itself, together with its documented interpretation bounds: a fitted n within 0.15 of 1 (0.85 to 1.15) reads as a Gaussian beam, n at or above 1.5 reads as flat-top, and the band between the two - 1.15 up to 1.5 - is deliberately left without a shape claim rather than an invented one. A fit whose n sits exactly at the underlying [0.5, 10] clamp is flagged at-boundary and receives no interpretation regardless of its numeric value, because a clamped fit only reports that the true optimum lies beyond where the search was allowed to go, not what shape the beam actually has. Alongside n, the same RMS-based quantities above are reported for both models side by side. Nothing in the comparison is phrased as evidence for one model over the other; it is a pair of honestly labeled numbers, not a verdict.

## Measured Numbers (Honest Record)

All of the following are measured, reproducible figures from this repository's own oracles, cited here rather than rounded to a nicer-looking number or invented.

- **SNR release curve after self-calibration and the v2.0 hardening pass** (300x240 synthetic frame, sigma 11x6, amplitude 100, 15 deterministic seeds): **15/15 seeds released at each of SNR 100, 50, 30 and 20 on the pinned seed base; 14-15/15 at SNR 100 and 13-15/15 at SNR 50, 30 and 20 over seed variation.** The curve is quoted as a band on purpose: measured over eight literal seed bases, a cell moves by up to two of fifteen without anything in the analyzer changing, so four point values would claim a precision the measurement does not have. The two middle cells were previously unpinned and are now pinned along with the outer two; the earlier record of this curve (15/15, 14/15, 13/15, 13/15) is one seed base's reading and is kept as the hard floor the oracles assert underneath. Before self-calibration, fixed thresholds suppressed 24 of 25 seeds at SNR 20.
- **Wing-heavy discrimination, honest limits**: a 10-percent-amplitude halo at 3x the core width stays suppressed (`alpha_inconsistent`) at SNR 20. A 1 percent halo on a small core (core sigma 4, halo sigma 16) is statistically unidentifiable at SNR 20 under any self-calibrating design that still meets the SNR-20 release figures above - two independent statistical derivations proved this the same way. The corresponding test fixture is pinned at a noise level where it stays caught, with a companion oracle confirming release once the noise is low enough to make the halo statistically visible again.
- **Second-beam detection**: at SNR 20 in a roughly 72000-pixel ROI, a genuine second beam needs about 26 percent of the main peak's amplitude to register as `multi_peak` (before self-calibration the effective figure was about 20 percent, but that lower number came from a threshold that also mistook pure noise for a second beam most of the time).
- **Super-Gaussian n = 0.8 is not an alpha discriminant**: the 2D-Gaussian fit absorbs the width difference, leaving only a 0.1-1.3 percent alpha-consistency delta - well inside the self-calibrated noise ceiling. Only much flatter-topped profiles (n <= 0.6) become testable this way (3.8-8.2 percent).
- **Pedestal bias**: because stage B measures on the fit-background-subtracted field, a flat 1 percent pedestal is absorbed before the moments are taken and the measured released D4sigma bias stays -0.001 percent; the pedestal indicator reports the absorbed level itself via `fittedBackgroundRelativeToPeak` rather than relying on a D4sigma bias that is no longer there to see.
- **Cut-profile phase bias on tilted anisotropic beams**: the horizontal/vertical cut sampler (an exact two-row pixel blend) reads a tilted anisotropic beam's width up to about +12.5 percent high at the worst half-pixel sub-pixel phase (measured: 12x1 px sigma at theta 0.3 rad, closed-form 7.694 px vs sampled 8.657 px). An alternative that anchors the sample walk at the sub-pixel through-point with full bilinear interpolation was implemented and independently re-measured before being reverted: it recovers the true peak position exactly but reads the SAME scene worse (+12.97 percent), because it forces every crossing sample through an extra reconstruction step the two-row blend does not need - a systematic 20x20 sub-pixel-phase sweep at a realistic 2:1 anisotropy found the existing two-row blend closer to the closed form in 380 of 400 phase combinations against 0 of 400 for the bilinear-anchored version. Left unchanged pending a different approach; a candidate for a later release.
- **Axis-profile bilinear ripple**: the rotated axis-profile sampler resamples the image with bilinear interpolation along an arbitrary angle, which carries its own small resampling ripple as the through-point's sub-pixel phase moves, on the order of 0.125 / sigmaMinor^2 percent - negligible for a well-resolved beam, growing as the minor axis narrows.
- **Radial-bin resolution limit**: the default 64-bin radial grid resolves a tight beam's 50-percent-power radius only to the width of bin 1 - a sigma-2 beam measures about 12 percent high against the pixel-sorted ground truth under the default bin count. This is a resolution limit of the diagnostic, not a defect; an adaptive bin count is a candidate for a later release.
- **Multi-peak separation reads the same fit it is checking**: the greedy separation distance (`2 * wEst`, `wEst = 2 * sigmaMajorFit`) is built from the single-Gaussian fit whose credibility the whole gate chain exists to question. When two comparable-amplitude beams sit close enough that the fit locks onto one lobe instead of resolving both, `wEst` reflects that one lobe, not the true separation - and by the precedence order, the alpha-consistency or fit-convergence gate typically suppresses the frame first anyway, under a less specific reason (`alpha_inconsistent` or `fit_not_converged`) than a dedicated two-beam label. `multi_peak` is reliable for a spatially separated, comparable-scale second peak the fit does not itself corrupt, not a general two-beam detector.
- **Ellipticity is pixel-space unless the physical variant is read**: the analyzer reports two independent ellipticity numbers - `ellipticity` (`sigmaMinorPx / sigmaMajorPx` of the released stage-B fit, in PIXEL units) and `ellipticityPhysical` (the same ratio taken from the released physical widths, in micrometers, once a pixel-pitch calibration is set) - deliberately never derived from one another. Under an anisotropic pixel pitch they diverge: measured, an 11/11 px sigma pair under a 3.45/1.15 um pitch (3:1 ratio) is pixel-round (`ellipticity` = 1.000) while the true physical ellipticity is 1/3, a +200 percent difference. Reading the pixel-space number as a physical roundness claim under an anisotropic pitch is the documented pitfall `ellipticityPhysical` exists to avoid.
- **Determinism**: identical input - same pixels, same configuration - produces bit-identical release thresholds and therefore a bit-identical release decision and number, every time on the same platform; across operating systems the iterative fit can move in its last bits through the platform's `Math.pow` (see the Determinism section), never in the released rounded numbers.
